17. How to Read the Codebase: A Developer's Roadmap

Why should I care?

This repository is a code-first learning tool. Instead of getting lost in lines of PyTorch boilerplate, you should approach the codebase in a logical order. This guide provides a step-by-step reading roadmap, detailing why each file exists, what to focus on, what to ignore initially, and what interview concepts it teaches.

The Reading Roadmap

flowchart LR Step1["1. model.py (The Engine Blueprint)"] --> Step2["2. generate.py (Autoregressive Test)"] Step2 --> Step3["3. train.py (Optimization Loop)"] Step3 --> Step4["4. data.py (Data Stream)"] Step4 --> Step5["5. hellaswag.py (Evaluation Test)"]

1. model.py

  • Why it exists: Defines the network architecture layers, parameter blocks, initialization scales, and weight-tying properties.
  • What to focus on:
    • The unified projection and reshape split math in CausalSelfAttention.
    • The residual additions and LayerNorm ordering in Block.forward().
    • Weight initialization scale adjustments in GPT._init_weights().
  • What to ignore initially: The Hugging Face weight loading helper function from_pretrained(). It is full of naming translations and Conv1D transpositions that are secondary to understanding core tensor flows.
  • Interview Concepts Taught: Self-attention equation, Pre-LN vs Post-LN stability, and Weight Tying parameter reductions.

2. generate.py

  • Why it exists: Runs a logit parity check to verify that our custom architecture yields identical math to official OpenAI GPT-2 weights. Also provides a text generation shell.
  • What to focus on: The logit validation loop run_logit_parity_test(). It is a perfect demonstration of loading models, setting evaluation configurations, and checking absolute delta thresholds between floating-point matrices.
  • What to ignore initially: Command-line parser setups in the main() block.
  • Interview Concepts Taught: Autoregressive text sampling, Temperature scaling mechanics, and Top-K distribution cropping.

3. train.py

  • Why it exists: Implements the production-grade optimization loop to train our local model, handling precision, learning rate warmup, and scaling.
  • What to focus on:
    • Optimizer decay group splitting in configure_optimizers().
    • Gradient accumulation loops in the training step block in main().
    • Mixed precision autocasting and gradient norm clipping constraints.
  • What to ignore initially: The validation loss text logging and sample generation blocks executed every 20 steps. Focus strictly on the gradient step block.
  • Interview Concepts Taught: Gradient Accumulation, Weight Decay parameter groupings, and Gradient Clipping protection.

4. data.py

  • Why it exists: Tokenizes the input dataset using tiktoken and handles slicing input matrices $x$ and shifted targets $y$.
  • What to focus on: The index slicing and target shifts in DataLoaderLite.next_batch(). Observe how target offsets are generated.
  • What to ignore initially: File reading encoding details and epoch count printouts.
  • Interview Concepts Taught: Causal target shifts and batch memory layout.

5. hellaswag.py

  • Why it exists: Downloads a validation subset of HellaSwag and evaluates model completions to measure accuracy.
  • What to focus on: The log-likelihood average calculation block in evaluate_hellaswag(). Notice how we slice outputs to align only with the suffix completion token logits.
  • What to ignore initially: Dataset download helper functions and curl requests.
  • Interview Concepts Taught: Commonsense reasoning benchmarks and Log-Likelihood completion evaluations.
