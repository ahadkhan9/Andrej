02. Attention: Causal Multi-Head Self-Attention

Why should I care?

Self-attention is the core engine of the transformer. Unlike older architectures (like RNNs or CNNs) which process sequences sequentially or within a fixed window, self-attention allows any token in a sequence to connect directly with any other token, regardless of distance. Understanding the mathematics and complexity of attention is essential for managing memory footprints (KV Cache) and optimizing inference speeds when deploying models to production.

Level 1 — Intuition & Mental Model

Imagine you are reading a book and encounter the word "it". To understand what "it" refers to, your eyes scan back across the sentence to locate the noun it represents. Self-attention performs this process mathematically:

  • Each token generates a Query (Q): "What target information am I looking for?"
  • Each token generates a Key (K): "What description do I offer to matching queries?"
  • Each token generates a Value (V): "What is my actual content?"
By taking the dot product of a token's Query with all tokens' Keys, the model calculates attention weights (similarity scores). These weights determine how much of each token's Value is mixed into the current token's representation.

Level 2 — Engineering View

In model.py:L70-L142, the CausalSelfAttention class implements this behavior:

  1. Unified Projection: A single linear layer (self.c_attn) projects the input tensor $X$ from shape [B, T, C] to [B, T, 3C], containing $Q$, $K$, and $V$ concatenated together.
  2. Split & Transpose: We split the projected tensor into $Q, K, V$ and reshape them to [B, H, T, D] (where $H$ is the number of heads and $D$ is the head dimension) to run calculations in parallel across all heads.
  3. Scaled Dot Product: We calculate similarity scores by multiplying $Q$ and $K^T$. Using torch.nn.functional.scaled_dot_product_attention, this step runs highly optimized **FlashAttention** kernels under the hood.

Level 3 — Mathematical View

Given the head matrices $Q, K, V \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times D}$: $$A = \text{Softmax}\left( \frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{D}} + M \right) V$$ Where $M$ is the causal mask matrix that prevents tokens from attending to future positions: $$M_{i,j} = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } i \geq j \\ -\infty & \text{if } i < j \end{cases}$$

4. Build Intuition through Numbers

Let's calculate attention manually for a sequence of 2 tokens, $A$ and $B$, with head dimension $D=2$. Assume the input embedding vectors are: $$X_A = [1, 0], \quad X_B = [0, 1]$$ Assume the projection weight matrices are identity matrices (to keep calculations simple): $$W_q = W_k = W_v = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$ This gives: $$Q = X \cdot W_q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad K = X \cdot W_k = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad V = X \cdot W_v = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 1: Compute Similarity Scores ($Q K^T$)

$$Q K^T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 2: Scale by $1 / \sqrt{D}$ (where $D=2, \sqrt{2} \approx 1.41$):

$$A_{scaled} = \frac{1}{1.41} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \approx \begin{pmatrix} 0.71 & 0.0 \\ 0.0 & 0.71 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 3: Apply Causal Mask ($M$)

$$A_{masked} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.71 & 0.0 \\ 0.0 & 0.71 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -\infty \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0.71 & -\infty \\ 0.0 & 0.71 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 4: Apply Softmax along each row

$$\text{Row 1}: \text{Softmax}([0.71, -\infty]) = [1.0, 0.0]$$ $$\text{Row 2}: \text{Softmax}([0.0, 0.71]) = \left[ \frac{e^{0}}{e^{0} + e^{0.71}}, \frac{e^{0.71}}{e^{0} + e^{0.71}} \right] \approx [0.33, 0.67]$$ $$\text{Attention Weights } W = \begin{pmatrix} 1.0 & 0.0 \\ 0.33 & 0.67 \end{pmatrix}$$

Step 5: Multiply by Values ($V$)

$$\text{Output} = W \cdot V = \begin{pmatrix} 1.0 & 0.0 \\ 0.33 & 0.67 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1.0 & 0.0 \\ 0.33 & 0.67 \end{pmatrix}$$ Notice how token $A$ only attends to itself (output $[1, 0]$), while token $B$ attends to both token $A$ and itself (output $[0.33, 0.67]$).

5. What happens if we remove causal masking?

If we remove causal masking (i.e. omitting the upper-triangular $-\infty$ mask step), the model becomes a bidirectional transformer (like BERT) rather than an autoregressive decoder. If you train the model without causal masking, it will learn to look ahead to the target tokens (which are simply the inputs shifted by one index). The model will achieve a near-zero training loss by simply copying the next token from the input, but will fail to generate coherent text during inference when future tokens are not available.
